# Voltway V2 Owner's Manual

Thank you for choosing the Voltway V2. This manual covers everyday use, charging, assist modes, error codes and maintenance. Read the safety section before your first ride.

## Specifications

The V2 uses a 250 W mid-drive motor with 85 Nm of peak torque and a removable 540 Wh battery integrated in the down tube. Assistance stops at 25 km/h. The bike weighs 23.4 kg including the battery, and the maximum total load (rider, luggage and bike) is 140 kg. Frame sizes are S (riders 155–170 cm), M (168–182 cm) and L (180–195 cm).

## Battery and charging

### Charging

Use only the supplied 4 A Voltway charger. A full charge from empty takes about 4.5 hours, and charging from 20% to 80% takes about 2 hours. You can charge the battery on the bike or removed from it. Charge indoors at temperatures between 10 °C and 30 °C; below 0 °C the battery management system blocks charging to protect the cells.

The charger LED is red while charging and turns green when the battery is full. If the LED blinks red, unplug the charger, wait one minute and try again. If it keeps blinking, the charger or battery may be faulty and you should contact support.

### Range

Range depends on assist mode, rider weight, terrain, tyre pressure and temperature. On flat roads with a 75 kg rider, typical range is 130 km in Eco, 95 km in Tour, 70 km in Sport and 50 km in Turbo. Cold weather can reduce range by up to 30%, because the cells deliver less energy at low temperatures.

### Storage and battery care

If you will not ride for more than a month, store the battery at 50–70% charge in a dry place between 10 °C and 20 °C, and top it up every three months. Storing a battery fully charged or completely empty for long periods shortens its life. Never store the battery in direct sunlight or in a car in summer.

A battery typically keeps at least 80% of its capacity after 800 full charge cycles. You can check battery health in the app under Bike > Battery > Health.

## Assist modes

The V2 has four assist modes, selected with the plus and minus buttons on the left handlebar remote. Eco gives 50% assistance and is best for long, flat rides. Tour gives 120% and is the balanced everyday mode. Sport gives 200% and suits hilly commutes. Turbo gives 300% and the full 85 Nm of torque for steep climbs and heavy loads.

Walk mode helps you push the bike at up to 6 km/h. Hold the minus button for 2 seconds while standing next to the bike, then keep holding the plus button to move.

## Riding safely

Before every ride, check that the brakes work, the tyres are inflated to the pressure printed on the sidewall (usually 2.5 to 3.5 bar for the V2's 2.4-inch tyres), the battery is locked into the frame, and the quick-release on the front wheel is closed. A battery that is not fully locked can rattle loose over bumps and interrupt power suddenly, which is dangerous in traffic.

Electric assist makes it easy to reach higher average speeds than on a normal bicycle, and other road users often underestimate how fast you are approaching. Ride defensively at junctions, use the integrated lights even during the day, and leave more braking distance than you would on an unassisted bike, especially in the wet. The V2's hydraulic disc brakes are powerful, so practise emergency stops in a quiet place before riding in traffic. Apply both brakes together and shift your weight back to avoid going over the handlebars.

Turbo mode delivers a strong push from a standstill. Start in Eco or Tour when you are on a slope, in a crowded area, or carrying a passenger seat with a child, and switch up once you are moving. Never ride with headphones that block outside sound, and always wear a helmet. In Morocco and most EU countries, children under 14 may not ride a pedelec on public roads.

When riding at night, check that the rear light is visible and keep the front light aimed slightly downwards so it does not dazzle oncoming traffic. The lights are powered by the main battery and stay on for about 2 hours after the display shows 0% assist remaining, so you are never left without lights on the way home.

## Display and error codes

The colour display shows speed, assist mode, battery level and remaining range. When the system detects a fault, it shows an error code. Note the code before turning the bike off, because some codes disappear after a restart.

| Code | Meaning | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E01 | Speed sensor signal lost | Check that the magnet on the rear wheel is aligned with the sensor. |
| E03 | Battery temperature out of range | Stop riding and let the battery cool down or warm up. |
| E05 | Motor overheated | Ride in a lower assist mode for 10 minutes. |
| E07 | Controller communication fault | Restart the bike. If it persists, contact support. |
| E09 | Torque sensor fault | Do not ride with assist; contact a service centre. |
| E12 | Battery not recognised | Remove and reinsert the battery, then clean the contacts. |

## Maintenance schedule

Clean the chain and drivetrain every 200 km, or after riding in rain or dust, and lubricate it with a dry or wet lube depending on conditions. Check tyre pressure weekly. Have the bike inspected at an authorised service centre after the first 500 km, when cables and bolts settle, and then every 3,000 km or once a year, whichever comes first.

Brake pads usually last 1,500 to 3,000 km depending on terrain. Replace them when the pad material is thinner than 1 mm. The chain should be replaced when a chain checker shows 0.5% wear, usually around 2,500 km on an e-bike, because the motor's torque wears chains faster than on a normal bicycle.

Keep the firmware up to date through the Voltway app, as updates improve range estimates and fix known issues. Do not install third-party firmware; it voids the warranty on the motor and controller.
